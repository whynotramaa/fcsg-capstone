\abstractText{%
Archival photographs usually suffer from several degradations at once:
optical blur, loss of resolution, sensor or film noise and lossy JPEG
compression. Each affects a different part of the image spectrum. This
project studies whether a very small restoration network can exploit that
structure. The proposed network, FCSG-Net, splits the degraded image into
low, mid and high frequency bands with fixed, soft radial masks in the
Fourier domain, processes every band with three shared convolutional experts
of kernel size $7\times7$, $5\times5$ and $3\times3$, and uses a lightweight
gate to select the two most suitable experts for each band at every pixel.
The model has 97,088 parameters and costs 9.39 GFLOPs for a $256\times256$
input. We train FCSG-Net, DnCNN and two FFDNet variants from scratch on the
same synthetic composite degradation of DIV2K images, using one schedule and
one evaluation protocol, after verifying the implementation and reproducing
the published FFDNet result to within 0.01\,dB. On 100 validation images
FCSG-Net reaches 27.24\,dB PSNR, against 27.83\,dB for DnCNN and 28.19\,dB
for blind FFDNet, while using 82.6\% fewer parameters than DnCNN. Its low
FLOP count does not translate into speed: on a Tesla T4 GPU it is the slowest
of the four models. The learned routing is spatially varying and tracks the
noise level, with within-image correlations up to 0.81, and overriding the
gate's selections at inference costs 0.37\,dB on average. Frequency-band
routing thus gives a small model inspectable behaviour, but within this
budget it does not make the model more accurate or faster than simple
convolutional baselines.
}
